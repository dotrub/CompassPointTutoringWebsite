Areas of Focus

High School Math

As math grows more complex in higher grades, students can start to feel lost, as gaps in their skills accumulate from earlier years.

Whether it's for Pre-Calculus, Foundations of Math, or Workplace Math, I help students find success while building up those fundamental skills, encouraging confidence and independent problem solving along the way.

Primary School Math

Early math education shapes how students feel about learning for years to come.

I place a strong emphasis on critical skills like computation, number sense, and problem-solving while helping students develop confidence and resilience. Laying strong foundations in primary school paves the way to achievement later on.

Online Courses

Online learning offers flexibility, but many students struggle to stay organized, motivated, and engaged without consistent support.

I help students stay on track with assignments, prepare for tests, and avoid falling behind, all while making sure they genuinely understand the material instead of rushing through coursework.

Science & Technology

I’m a former software developer with a longstanding interest in computers, science, and technology.

I provide support for high school science courses including chemistry and physics, and I enjoy incorporating coding and technology into learning whenever possible. I can also help students learn how to use modern AI tools thoughtfully and effectively as part of their education.

About Dylan Otruba

Math causes a lot of complicated feelings for people, and that starts in school. Students accumulate gaps in their skillset, they experience challenges that feel insurmountable, they get bored with the repetitive practice. Eventually many of them conclude that math is simply beyond them. My approach as a tutor is centred around proving the opposite: that competence, and then confidence, are possible for anyone with practice and support.

I started my career as a software developer, with a degree in Computer Science from the University of British Columbia. I've always been drawn to understanding how things work. Whether I'm solving a programming problem, learning a new subject, or helping a student work through a difficult concept, I enjoy breaking complex ideas into smaller pieces and finding the connections between them. Many students learn how to follow the steps without ever really understanding why those steps work. I want my students to understand the ideas behind the math.

When it comes to working with students, finding an individual approach for each person is one of my specialities. I emphasize a calm, friendly environment where it's safe to explore and ask questions without judgment. A lot of progression in math is halted by anxiety and insecurity, as gaps in foundational skills build up over time. If those gaps are identified and strengthened, learning becomes much easier. I find that students are more capable of learning than they usually think; most students just need to have some deeper gap or misunderstanding addressed first.

Overall, I want to see my students move forward with the confidence that they can learn whatever they set their minds to, if they have resilience and determination. Watching a student move from the mindset of "I can't do this" to "this is difficult, but I can do it" is the greatest sign of success. Along the way, some of my favourite moments happen when a student begins making their own connections, asking their own questions, and discovering that learning can be genuinely enjoyable. Curiosity is difficult to cultivate when math feels frustrating or overwhelming, but once students begin to understand the material and trust their own abilities, it emerges naturally. If a student leaves a lesson feeling a little more capable, a little more confident, and a little more curious than when they arrived, then I consider that time well spent.
